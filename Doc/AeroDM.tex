\begin{abstract}
This document provides a comprehensive analysis of DACS (Diffusion-based Aerobatics with CBF-Guided Sampling), an enhanced transformer-based diffusion model for generating aerobatic UAV trajectories in urban environments. The model incorporates obstacle awareness through MLP-based obstacle encoding, Control Barrier Function (CBF) guidance for safety guarantees, and multi-modal conditional inputs including target waypoints, maneuver styles, historical observations, and obstacle information. We examine the enhanced architecture, CBF-guided sampling methodology, performance characteristics, and propose improvement strategies for urban air mobility applications.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The DACS model represents a significant advancement in UAV trajectory generation for urban environments, combining diffusion processes with transformer-based architecture and Control Barrier Function guidance. The model is designed to handle complex aerobatic maneuvers while maintaining physical constraints, obstacle avoidance, and maneuver style consistency in cluttered urban settings.

\section{Model Architecture Analysis}

\subsection{Overall Framework}

The DACS framework consists of four main components:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Obstacle-Aware Diffusion Transformer}: Core generative model with obstacle encoding
    \item \textbf{CBF-Guided Diffusion Process}: Safety-guaranteed sampling with barrier functions
    \item \textbf{Multi-modal Conditioning System}: Integrated obstacle, target, and style processing
    \item \textbf{Obstacle Encoder MLP}: Neural network for obstacle representation learning
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsubsection{Diffusion Process with CBF Guidance}

The forward diffusion process follows the standard formulation:

\begin{equation}
q(x_t|x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)I)
\end{equation}

where $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_s$ and $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$.

The reverse process is enhanced with CBF guidance:

\begin{equation}
p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1}; \mu_\theta(x_t, t) - \gamma_t \nabla V(x_t), \sigma_t^2 I)
\end{equation}

where $V(x_t)$ is the control barrier function and $\gamma_t$ is the guidance strength.

\subsubsection{Control Barrier Function}

The CBF for multiple spherical obstacles:

\begin{equation}
V(x) = \sum_{\tau=1}^{T} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{obs}} \max(0, r_i - \|p_\tau - c_i\|)^2
\end{equation}

where $p_\tau$ is the position at time $\tau$, $c_i$ and $r_i$ are obstacle centers and radii.

\subsubsection{Enhanced Transformer Architecture}

The diffusion transformer employs obstacle-aware conditioning:

\begin{equation}
h_0 = \text{InputProj}(x) + \text{PosEnc} + \text{EnhancedCondEmbed}(t, target, action, obstacles)
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
h_l = \text{TransformerLayer}(h_{l-1}, h_{l-1}), \quad l=1,\dots,L
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_0 = \text{OutputProj}(h_L)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Component Details}

\subsubsection{Obstacle Encoder MLP}

The obstacle encoder processes multiple obstacles:

\begin{equation}
e_{obs} = \text{GlobalObstacleEncoder}\left(\text{AttentionAggregation}\left(\text{ObstacleMLP}(o_i)\right)_{i=1}^{N_{obs}}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Enhanced Condition Embedding}

Multi-modal conditioning with obstacle fusion:

\begin{equation}
e_{cond} = \text{FusionLayer}([e_t; e_{target}; e_{action}; e_{obstacles}])
\end{equation}

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:training-objective}

The training objective below follows the current implementation of
\texttt{AeroDMLoss} in \texttt{AeroDM\_ObstacleAware\_Training\_Test.py}.
The model predicts the clean trajectory $\hat{x}_0$ from a noisy input at a
random diffusion step; supervision is applied to this reconstructed trajectory.
Let $B$ be the batch size and $T$ the prediction horizon. Each state contains
scalar speed $s$, position $p\in\mathbb{R}^3$, velocity $v\in\mathbb{R}^3$,
and three attitude components $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^3$. A tilde denotes a
feature normalized using the training mean and standard deviation, and a hat
denotes a prediction. Unless stated otherwise, the losses use normalized states.

\subsubsection{State Reconstruction and Endpoint Supervision}
Define the unweighted reconstruction terms as
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{p} &= \frac{1}{BT}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}
 \|\hat{\tilde p}_{b,\tau}-\tilde p_{b,\tau}\|_2^2, \\
\mathcal{L}_{e} &= \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
 \|\hat{\tilde p}_{b,T}-\tilde p_{b,T}\|_2^2, \\
\mathcal{L}_{v} &= \frac{1}{BT}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}
 \|\hat{\tilde v}_{b,\tau}-\tilde v_{b,\tau}\|_2^2, \\
\mathcal{L}_{s} &= \frac{1}{BT}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}
 (\hat{\tilde s}_{b,\tau}-\tilde s_{b,\tau})^2, \\
\mathcal{L}_{\eta} &= \frac{1}{3BT}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}
 \|\hat{\tilde\eta}_{b,\tau}-\tilde\eta_{b,\tau}\|_2^2.
\end{align}
The position, endpoint, and velocity terms sum three per-axis mean squared
errors, whereas the attitude term averages over its three channels. The same
position scale factor $w$ is applied to all three axes; there is no additional
vertical-axis weight. The endpoint term supervises the final ground-truth
position. Removing the endpoint-error threshold from evaluation does not remove
this training term. The implementation sets $\mathcal{L}_{v}=0$ for $T\leq1$.

\subsubsection{Boundary Continuity and Trajectory Smoothness}
Let $\tilde p^h_{b,-1}$ and $\tilde p^h_{b,0}$ be the last two historical
positions. With equally spaced historical and predicted samples, boundary
continuity is encouraged by matching their displacements:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{c}=\frac{1}{3B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\left\|(\hat{\tilde p}_{b,1}-\tilde p^h_{b,0})
 -(\tilde p^h_{b,0}-\tilde p^h_{b,-1})\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
This term is zero when fewer than two historical samples are available.
Smoothness within the predicted horizon is penalized using second differences:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{a}=\frac{1}{3B(T-2)}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{\tau=2}^{T-1}
\left\|\hat{\tilde p}_{b,\tau+1}-2\hat{\tilde p}_{b,\tau}
 +\hat{\tilde p}_{b,\tau-1}\right\|_2^2,\qquad T\geq3.
\end{equation}
For shorter horizons this term is zero. It is a normalized-position
second-difference penalty, without division by $\Delta t^4$, rather than a
mean squared physical acceleration or an acceleration-tracking objective.

\subsubsection{Kinematic Consistency}
Positions and velocity channels are first denormalized. When history is
available, its final state is prepended to the predictions so that the boundary
interval is also constrained. For each consecutive pair in this augmented
sequence, define
\begin{align}
 a_{b,\tau} &= \frac{v_{b,\tau}-v_{b,\tau-1}}{\Delta t}, \\
 r_{b,\tau} &= \left[p_{b,\tau}-p_{b,\tau-1}
 -v_{b,\tau-1}\Delta t-\tfrac12 a_{b,\tau}\Delta t^2\right]
 \oslash\sigma_p, \\
\mathcal{L}_{k} &= \frac{1}{3BK}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{\tau=1}^{K}
 \|r_{b,\tau}\|_2^2.
\end{align}
Here the states are predicted except for the optional historical boundary
state, $\oslash$ is componentwise division, and $\sigma_p$ is the position
standard deviation, clamped below by $10^{-8}$. There are $K=T$ intervals
with history and $K=T-1$ otherwise; the loss is zero if no interval exists.
The update is equivalent to trapezoidal integration,
$p_{\tau}-p_{\tau-1}=\tfrac12(v_{\tau-1}+v_{\tau})\Delta t$.
The physical residual is normalized to the position-reconstruction scale.
Training supplies the normalization statistics; if both are omitted, the
implementation treats the supplied states as physical and uses unit scale.

\subsubsection{Optional Obstacle Penalty}
For sample $b$, let $\mathcal{O}_b$ contain spherical obstacles with centers
$c_{b,j}$ and radii $r_{b,j}$, and let $N=\sum_b|\mathcal{O}_b|$. Using
physical predicted positions and an absolute safety margin $m$ in meters,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{o}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{O}_b}
 \sum_{\tau=1}^{T}
 \left[\max\left(0,r_{b,j}+m-\|\hat p_{b,\tau}-c_{b,j}\|_2\right)\right]^2.
\end{equation}
The term is zero when disabled, when the required obstacle data or normalization
statistics are absent, or when $N=0$. The implemented denominator is the total
obstacle count $N=B\bar N$, without division by $T$; consequently, the penalty
accumulates over the prediction horizon.

\subsubsection{Total Objective and Current Weights}
The exact weighted objective, expressed using the unweighted terms above, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}={}&w^2\mathcal{L}_{p}
 +\lambda_e w\mathcal{L}_{e}+\lambda_v w\mathcal{L}_{v}
 +\lambda_{\mathrm{other}}(\mathcal{L}_{s}+\mathcal{L}_{\eta})\\
 &+\lambda_o\mathcal{L}_{o}+\lambda_c\mathcal{L}_{c}
 +\lambda_a\mathcal{L}_{a}+\lambda_k\mathcal{L}_{k}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The factor $w^2$ reflects the implementation: \texttt{xyz\_weight} multiplies
each position-axis loss and then multiplies their sum again in the total loss.
The endpoint and velocity terms each receive this factor only once.
The current configuration uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
w=1,\quad \lambda_e=100,\quad \lambda_v=0,\quad
\lambda_{\mathrm{other}}=1,\\
\lambda_o=1,\quad \lambda_c=5,\quad \lambda_a=1,\quad
\lambda_k=10,\quad \Delta t=0.1\,\mathrm{s},\quad m=0\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus direct velocity reconstruction is inactive, while velocity channels remain
constrained by kinematic consistency. Obstacle loss is currently disabled by
\texttt{use\_obstacle\_loss=False}, so its contribution is zero despite
$\lambda_o=1$. These are fixed weights; adaptive weighting is a proposed
extension, not part of the current training objective.

\section{Strengths and Innovations}

\subsection{Architectural Advantages}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Obstacle-Aware Generation}: MLP-based obstacle encoding integrated into transformer
    \item \textbf{CBF Safety Guarantees}: Formal safety verification through barrier functions
    \item \textbf{Multi-scale Conditioning}: Effective integration of temporal, spatial, behavioral, and obstacle constraints
    \item \textbf{Causal Attention}: Proper temporal modeling with masked self-attention

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enhanced Data Generation}: Diverse aerobatic maneuver simulation with urban obstacles
    \item \textbf{Progressive Normalization}: Dimension-aware normalization strategy

    \item \textbf{Obstacle-Aware Loss}: Optional explicit obstacle penalty (disabled in the current configuration)
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Improvement Areas}

\subsection{Architectural Limitations}

\subsubsection{Obstacle Representation}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Issue}: Fixed maximum number of obstacles (10)
    \item \textbf{Impact}: Cannot handle arbitrarily large obstacle sets
    \item \textbf{Solution}: Dynamic obstacle processing or graph neural networks
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Attention Mechanism}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Issue}: Full self-attention has $O(N^2)$ complexity
    \item \textbf{Impact}: Limits sequence length and training efficiency
    \item \textbf{Solution}: Implement sparse attention or linear attention variants
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Limitations}

\subsubsection{Loss Function Complexity}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Issue}: Multiple loss components require careful balancing
    \item \textbf{Impact}: Sensitive to hyperparameter tuning
    \item \textbf{Solution}: Adaptive loss weighting or multi-task learning
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{CBF Guidance Stability}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Issue}: Fixed guidance strength $\gamma_t$
    \item \textbf{Impact}: May cause training instability or over-constraint
    \item \textbf{Solution}: Learnable guidance scheduling or adaptive $\gamma_t$
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed Improvements}

\subsection{Architectural Enhancements}

\subsubsection{Dynamic Obstacle Processing}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Dynamic Obstacle Encoding}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{DynamicObstacleEncode}{$obstacles, k$}
\State $embeddings \gets \text{ObstacleMLP}(obstacles)$
\State $indices \gets \text{TopKByDistance}(embeddings, k)$
\State $selected \gets embeddings[indices]$
\State $aggregated \gets \text{AttentionAggregate}(selected)$
\Return $aggregated$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Hierarchical Transformer}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Hierarchical Transformer Architecture}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{HierarchicalEncode}{$x, levels$}
\State $patches \gets \text{Patchify}(x, patch\_size)$
\State $h \gets \text{LocalAttention}(patches)$
\For{$i \gets 1$ to $levels$}
\State $h \gets \text{GlobalAttention}(h)$
\State $h \gets \text{Downsample}(h)$
\EndFor
\Return $h$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Training Improvements}

\subsubsection{Adaptive CBF Guidance}

Learnable guidance scheduling:

\begin{equation}
\gamma_t = \gamma_{base} \cdot \sigma(W_t t + b_t)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Enhanced Loss Balancing}

As a future extension to the fixed weights in Section~\ref{sec:training-objective}, an uncertainty-weighted multi-task objective could be used:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \sum_{i=1}^{D} \frac{1}{2\sigma_i^2} \mathcal{L}_i + \log \sigma_i
\end{equation}

where $\sigma_i$ are learnable uncertainty parameters.

\subsubsection{Curriculum Learning}

Progressive difficulty scheduling:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Phase 1: Basic trajectory learning without obstacles
    \item Phase 2: Simple obstacle avoidance
    \item Phase 3: Complex urban environments with multiple obstacles
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Physical Constraints Integration}

\subsubsection{Enhanced Dynamics Constraints}

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{physics} = \lambda_{vel} \| \hat{v} - v_{gt} \|^2 + \lambda_{acc} \| \hat{a} - a_{gt} \|^2 + \lambda_{jerk} \| \hat{j} - j_{gt} \|^2
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{urban} = \lambda_{building} \sum \max(0, h_{min} - z_t) + \max(0, z_t - h_{max})
\end{equation}

\subsection*{A. Mathematical Derivations}

\subsubsection*{A.1 CBF-Guided Reverse Diffusion}

The CBF-guided reverse process derivation:

\begin{equation}
p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t) \propto p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t) \exp(-\gamma_t V(x_t))
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{A.2 Obstacle Distance Gradient}

Gradient of the barrier function:

\begin{equation}
\nabla V(x_t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{obs}} -2 \cdot \max(0, r_i - \|p_t - c_i\|) \cdot \frac{p_t - c_i}{\|p_t - c_i\|}
\end{equation}
